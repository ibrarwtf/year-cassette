One crank in the middle. Turn it clockwise to play forward and anticlockwise to rewind. When you change direction, the crank turns about a third of a turn before the paper moves: that's the idler swinging across to the other spool.

Year Cassette

A fully mechanical box with no electronics that replays a year of daily thermal prints: one 48 mm square per day, about 19.6 m of paper.

How the drive works

  1. Crank and sun gear share one shaft in the middle of the box, just behind the paper.
  2. Swing arm and idler gear. The arm pivots on the sun gear's shaft and rubs it lightly, so it swings whichever way the crank turns.
  3. Turning clockwise swings the idler into the left spool's gear, which winds paper onto the left spool (play).
  4. Turning anticlockwise swings it across into the right spool's gear, which winds paper back onto the right spool (rewind).
  5. The spool that isn't being driven is pulled along by the paper against a light printed brake, so the paper stays taut in both directions.

The spools aren't geared directly to each other on purpose. As paper moves across, one roll grows from 15 to 41 mm while the other shrinks, so their speeds keep changing (by up to 2.7×). A rigid link would tear the paper or let it go slack. Cassette decks solve this the same way.

Parts: 9 prints, no screws, no electronics

  1. Tray: floor with the three axle pins (two spools and the sun gear).
  2. Deck plate: drops in above the gears. The two guide posts and two springy brake tabs are printed in.
  3. Lid with a 124 × 64 mm window. It snaps on.
  4. Spool × 2, each with a 35-tooth gear printed at the bottom and a slot in the core for the paper end.
  5. Sun gear (15 teeth) with a tall D-shaft up through the lid.
  6. Swing arm with a built-in flex spring that presses it against the sun gear (that's the friction).
  7. Idler gear (20 teeth) on the end of the arm.
  8. Crank handle, press-fitted onto the D-shaft above the lid.

Gears are module 2 (large, chunky teeth that print reliably). One crank turn moves the paper 20 to 55 mm, depending on how full the take-up roll is: roughly one to two turns per day. The box is about 22 × 9 × 8 cm, and every part fits a Bambu A1 or P1S bed.

Printing the paper

The paper path only touches the back of the paper (guide posts) and its edges (spool flanges). The printed side never rubs, because thermal paper darkens with friction. Use long-life thermal paper for a roll you want to keep for years.
