Year Cassette

A year of memories on one roll of receipt paper, in a box you crank by hand to replay it. Fully mechanical: no electronics, no screws, no glue.

Why

Thermal receipt paper costs almost nothing, and a cheap receipt printer prints on it without ink. That raised a question: what would you print if paper came by the metre?

A year, one small square per day. The first plan was a wall collage, with 52 week-columns, each 7 days tall. Laid out properly it's three metres wide. Next came one framed month at a time, but that's 12 frames a year and 60 after five years. Too much stuff.

What we really wanted was one object per year. So the year became one long strip, about 19.6 m, the strip became a roll, and the roll went into a cassette. Turn the crank and the days scroll past the window. Turn it back to rewind.

How it works

  • One crank in the middle turns a small sun gear.
  • A swing arm carrying an idler gear follows the crank's direction. Clockwise it meshes with the left spool (play); anticlockwise it swings across to the right spool (rewind). It's the same trick cassette decks use.
  • The spool that isn't driven is pulled along by the paper against a light printed brake, so the paper stays taut both ways and can't jam.
  • The spools are never geared to each other: one roll grows while the other shrinks, so their speeds keep changing.

Print: PLA, 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 15% infill, no supports. Use 4 walls and a brim for the sun gear shaft. Three plates: (1) tray, (2) deck + lid, (3) everything else. Print spool_drive, spool and spool_cap twice each.

Assemble: spool drives and sun gear onto the floor pins, idler onto the swing arm, arm onto the sun shaft, then the deck, the paper roll, the spools and caps, the lid, and the crank. The build guide has the step-by-step version, how to make and load the paper roll, and a tuning table.

Status: prototype v1. It's modelled and checked (no collisions, every gear pair meshes), but nobody has printed it yet, so expect to tune a fit or two.

Making the paper

Print one 48 mm square per day on 58 mm thermal paper, with each picture rotated 90°, because the paper runs sideways through the window. Join the strips with tape on the back. Wind the year onto the right spool, printed side out, with Dec 31 at the core. Then lead Jan 1 around the front of the guide posts into the left spool. Use long-life thermal paper if you want it to last for years.
